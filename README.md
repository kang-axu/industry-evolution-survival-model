# Industry Evolution Survival Model (IESM)

> **Before transforming an industry, first understand what survives the transformation.**

IESM is the pre-transformation analysis branch of the **Industry Transformation Engine (ITE)**. It examines what happens to an industry when a new technological, infrastructural, or organizational paradigm begins replacing an old one.

## Why IESM Exists

Transformation work often begins with questions about adoption, automation, optimization, or new products. IESM starts one step earlier: whether the structure of the industry itself is changing.

If the underlying structure is shifting, improving the old structure may not reveal where the industry is heading. IESM is intended to provide that pre-intervention view.

## Core Problem

When a new paradigm replaces an old one, the original industry rarely disappears as a whole. Its participants, roles, assets, and profit pools can move in different directions.

IESM studies this survival and redistribution process: what remains, what weakens, what moves, and what emerges.

## Main Objects of Analysis

IESM examines the effects of a paradigm shift on:

- Players: companies, intermediaries, specialists, platforms, and operators.
- Roles: jobs and capabilities that disappear, shrink, transform, or become more valuable.
- Assets: the assets that decline, persist, or become newly important.
- Value: where profits and economic value move.
- Control points: where coordination, access, standards, or decision-making power move.

## Survival Branches

IESM uses survival branches to describe possible outcomes for parts of an existing industry, including disappearance, decline, migration, commoditization, infrastructure status, augmentation, and the creation of new roles or intermediaries.

These branches are categories for observation and comparison, not predictions that every transition will follow the same path.

## Historical Analogy

Historical cases provide a structured way to compare industry transitions. The goal is not to retell history, but to identify how survival, value, and control changed across different paradigm shifts.

## IESM and ITE

IESM comes before ITE. IESM assesses the changing industry structure; ITE uses that assessment to inform intervention and transformation strategy.

## Questions IESM Tries to Answer

- What in the current industry is likely to survive the transition?
- What may disappear, decline, migrate, or become infrastructure?
- Which roles, assets, intermediaries, and capabilities may become newly important?
- Where might value and control points move?
- What can historical transitions contribute to a current-industry assessment?
- What should ITE consider before intervening in an industry?

## Intended Use

IESM is intended for pre-transformation industry analysis. It provides a common structure for studying historical transitions, extracting reusable observations, and assessing current industries before designing interventions.

## Current Research Direction

The current work is to build comparable historical case studies, document their survival branches and value/control movements, and organize observations into a reusable pattern library. Initial cases include electrification, railways, automobiles, containerization, the internet, smartphones, cloud computing, and digital photography.

## Long-Term Goal

The long-term goal is to develop a disciplined research base that helps ITE understand an industry’s changing structure before defining how to transform it.

## Status

IESM V0.1 is initializing its framework, historical-case structure, research templates, and empty pattern and assessment spaces. Specific cross-industry conclusions have not yet been filled in.
